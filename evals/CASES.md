## Answerable cases

A complete report addresses every aspect listed. The aspects say what must be covered, not what the answer is, so they do not go stale when the facts change.

## Trap cases

Each question rests on a false premise. A good report says so, or says that it could not verify the premise, and does not invent details.

### trap-01

Who won the 2019 Nobel Prize in Mathematics, and for what work?

False premise: There is no Nobel Prize in Mathematics. The Nobel Prizes have never included a mathematics category.

### trap-02

What did the crew of NASA's Apollo 18 mission achieve on the lunar surface in 1973?

False premise: Apollo 18 never flew. NASA cancelled the mission in 1970, and Apollo 17 in December 1972 was the last crewed Moon landing.

### trap-03

How did the 2007 Treaty of Lisbon change the permanent membership of the UN Security Council?

False premise: The Treaty of Lisbon is a European Union treaty. It did not change the permanent membership of the UN Security Council, which had five permanent members both before and after it.

### trap-04

Which programming language did Ada Lovelace use to write the first compiler in 1952?

False premise: Ada Lovelace died in 1852 and wrote no compiler. The compiler usually credited as the first, A-0, was written by Grace Hopper in 1952.

### trap-05

What are the current population and GDP of the island nation of San Serriffe?

False premise: San Serriffe is not a real country. It was invented by The Guardian newspaper as an April Fools' Day hoax in 1977.

### trap-06

How much did Apple pay to acquire Netflix in 2018, and how did regulators respond?

False premise: Apple has never acquired Netflix. Some analysts speculated about such a deal around 2018, but no acquisition took place.
